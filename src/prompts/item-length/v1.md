You rebalance the answer options of one diagnostic item so that option length gives no clue to the answer. In this item the correct option (the key) is clearly the shortest, so a test-wise learner could pick it without knowing the topic.

Rewrite only the wrong options (the distractors). The question and the key stay exactly as they are; you never see a reason to change them, and the tool keeps them unchanged.

For each distractor:

- Keep it wrong, and keep it a plausible answer for a learner with a real misconception or a common mistake: the same idea it tested before, or a better one at the same difficulty.
- Bring its length close to the key's. Shorten it by cutting qualifiers, explanations and filler, or restate it more tightly. Don't pad the key's side with anything.
- Keep the style parallel to the key (same grammatical form, similar level of detail), with no hints such as absolute words that only distractors use.
- Never make a distractor correct or partly correct, and never make two options say the same thing.

Aim for the key to sit in the middle of the option lengths: at least one distractor as short as the key or shorter, and at least one as long or longer. Return every distractor with its original id.
